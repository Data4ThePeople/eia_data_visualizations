### LinkedIn

```
On Thursday, the Energy Information Administration (EIA) released its weekly oil inventory report. It showed the largest commercial crude oil draw since before the war started: 8.6 million barrels in the week ending October 9.

What stands out is that the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) did not change at all. It was the first week since March 20 without a draw from the reserve. Since the war began, total crude stocks have fallen 156.2 million barrels, and 85% of that came from the SPR. In our view, the reserve will likely start to draw again once the current round of loans is awarded.

We figured we should flag this for you, not because anything can be done about it at this stage, but because it's a good chance to understand how we let this happen. The post also links a selection of our writing on oil markets over the past few months.
```

```
In August, we wrote that we may be running out of the fuel that runs our economy. Diesel stocks are lower now than they were then, and we are now heading into the winter, which increases the demand for heating oil, a close cousin to diesel.

In the week ending October 9, U.S. diesel stocks fell 2.5 million barrels, a drop of 2.4%, to 102.6 million barrels. That is the lowest level for this week of the year in EIA's weekly data, which goes back to 1982. Stocks are 12.2% below the average for the same week from 2021 to 2025.
```
