## 3. Body, part one

```
Yesterday, the Energy Information Administration (EIA) released its weekly oil inventory report, a day later than usual because of the Columbus Day holiday. It covers the week ending October 9.

The report showed the largest commercial crude oil draw since before the war started. We figured we should flag this for you, not because anything can be done about it at this stage, but because it's a good chance to understand how we let this happen. To assist you with that, we've included links to a selection of our writing on oil markets over the past few months. We do this not to take a victory lap. There are no winners with what is going on here. It's to illustrate how valuable it is to have a basic level of education on things like how a refinery works. I constantly wonder: if we had all known how a refinery worked back in March, would we have done something differently to avoid this fate? I'll never know the answer, but if D4TP's vision plays out, there will be a day when we all see through the raging tempest of noise to a shared truth.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
U.S. crude oil stocks fell 8.6 million barrels last week, and all of it came from commercial stocks. It was the largest weekly drop in commercial crude since the war began on February 28. What stands out is that the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) did not change at all. Last week was the first week since March 20 without a draw from the reserve.

Diesel stocks fell to 102.6 million barrels. That is the lowest level for this week of the year in EIA's weekly data, which goes back to 1982. And we are now heading into the winter, which increases the demand for heating oil, a close cousin to diesel. Gasoline looks better.

The post has two animated GIFs that, in under 20 seconds each, replay the past 45 years of diesel stocks (back to 1982) and the past 37 years of gasoline stocks (back to 1990). We also explain why we think the reserve will likely start to draw again, and link to our coverage of the 2026 energy crisis, from refinery margins to sulfur.
```
