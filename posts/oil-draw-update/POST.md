# Oil Update: The Biggest Commercial Crude Draw of the War

The Energy Information Administration (EIA) released its weekly oil inventory report on Thursday, October 15, a day later than usual because of the Columbus Day holiday. It covers the week ending October 9. Here is a quick update on crude oil and diesel, followed by a guide to everything we have written on the 2026 energy crisis.

## Crude oil: a big draw, and none of it from the reserve

U.S. crude oil stocks fell 8.6 million barrels last week. That figure combines two parts: commercial stocks, held by companies, and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), held by the government. All of last week's drop came from commercial stocks. It was the largest weekly drop in commercial crude since the war began on February 28.

It was not the largest drop in total crude, though. The chart below shows the weekly change in both parts since the war began.

*Weekly change in U.S. crude oil stocks, commercial and Strategic Petroleum Reserve, weeks ending March 6 to October 9, 2026. Source: EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report.*

The biggest weeks came in May and June, when the SPR was releasing the most oil. The largest was the week ending May 15, when total stocks fell 17.8 million barrels, and 56% of that came from the reserve. Last week ranks 11th of the 32 weeks since the war began. It was the largest total drop since the week ending July 24.

What stands out is that the SPR did not change at all. Last week was the first week since March 20 without a draw from the reserve. Since the war began, total crude stocks have fallen 156.2 million barrels, and 132.5 million of that, or 85%, came from the SPR. As we covered in [Meet the Speculative Petroleum Reserve](https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/spr-exchange), the government has been lending that oil to companies in rounds. In our view, the reserve will likely start to draw again once the current round of loans is awarded.

## Diesel: the lowest for this time of year since 1982

Diesel stocks fell 2.5 million barrels last week, a drop of 2.4%, to 102.6 million barrels. (EIA counts diesel and heating oil together as distillate fuel oil.) That is the lowest level for this week of the year in EIA's weekly data, which goes back to 1982. The next lowest was 106.1 million barrels in 2022. Stocks are 12.2% below the average for the same week from 2021 to 2025.

## About the data

All figures come from EIA's Weekly Petroleum Status Report: commercial crude oil (series WCESTUS1), crude oil in the SPR (WCSSTUS1), total crude oil including the SPR (WCRSTUS1) and distillate fuel oil (WDISTUS1). Weekly commercial figures are estimates from a survey of companies, so any one week's change can be off, and EIA's monthly data later replaces them. The SPR figure is a count from the Department of Energy. "Since the war began" means the 32 weeks ending March 6 through October 9, 2026. For diesel, "this week of the year" compares the week ending October 9 with the week closest to the same date in every year since 1982.
